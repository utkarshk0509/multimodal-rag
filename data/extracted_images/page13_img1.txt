**4. Core Engineering Conclusion Represented by the Visualization:**

The visualization clearly demonstrates that **using a "Predicted TLE" method provides significantly superior accuracy and stability in estimating both the position and velocity of the IRIDIUM 7 satellite compared to relying on an "Outdated TLE" over a several-hour period.**

Specifically:
*   For **position**, the predicted TLE maintains an RMSE an order of magnitude lower (around 0.1 km) than the outdated TLE (which grows from 0.55 km to nearly 0.8 km).
*   For **velocity**, the predicted TLE also maintains a consistently lower and more stable RMSE (around 0.0002 km/s) compared to the outdated TLE (which grows from 0.00058 km/s to nearly 0.00067 km/s).

This highlights the critical importance of utilizing updated or accurately predicted orbital elements for precise satellite navigation, tracking, and mission planning. Outdated TLEs quickly lead to unacceptable levels of error, underscoring the necessity of continuous TLE updates or robust predictive models to maintain accurate orbital state knowledge.
